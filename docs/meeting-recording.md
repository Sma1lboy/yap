# Meeting recording

One shortcut (right ⌘ + Space by default) starts a long recording of the microphone and the sound from other apps; only ✓ in the meeting panel stops it (the shortcut then just brings the panel forward). When a call app starts using the microphone, Yap can also offer to start it (call detection, below); it never starts or stops a recording by itself. Yap transcribes while the meeting goes on and writes notes when it stops. Nothing is pasted: the result is one History entry and a small window with the notes.

## How it works

| Step | What happens | Code |
|---|---|---|
| Microphone ("Me") | Its own `CoreAudioRecorder` on the current recording device. It doesn't go through dictation's `Recorder`, so the output isn't muted and media isn't paused. Dictation during a meeting also skips both. | `MeetingRecorder.Session`, `Recorder.startRecording` |
| System audio ("Others") | A Core Audio process tap on every process except Yap (`CATapDescription(stereoGlobalTapButExcludeProcesses:)`), inside a private aggregate device built on the current output device, read with an IOProc. Downmixed and resampled to 16 kHz mono with `PCMResampler`. When the default output changes (AirPods), the aggregate device is rebuilt. | `SystemAudioTap` |
| Timeline | Both channels are written to `mic.wav` / `system.wav` and kept on the wall clock: if a channel falls more than 0.5 s behind (device switch, stalled device), the gap is filled with silence, so both timelines stay aligned. | `MeetingRecorder.Channel` |
| Pieces | Each channel is cut at the quietest 100 ms between 20 and 28 s. Pieces below about −50 dBFS are dropped (Whisper invents text for silence). Leading silence is trimmed, so the timestamp is where speech starts. | `MeetingChunker` |
| Transcription | One piece at a time, in order, through `TranscriptionServiceRegistry` with the mode's model and language as they were when the meeting started, the same entry point dictation uses (local Whisper, cloud, Yap Cloud…). A piece that fails keeps its line in the transcript, `[01:02] Others: (This part couldn't be transcribed.)` (`failed` in `segments.json`); the panel and the History row say "N parts couldn't be transcribed". The notes are written from the other lines. With local Whisper each piece takes a turn with dictations (`WhisperModelManager.withContext`): a dictation on another model during the meeting waits for the piece being decoded, then loads its model, and the next piece loads the meeting's model back (see [local-models.md](local-models.md#transcriptions-that-overlap)). | `MeetingRecorder.Transcriber`, `MeetingNotes.failedMarker` |
| Echo | Without headphones the other side comes out of the speakers into the microphone and would be transcribed twice, under "Others" and "Me". After the last piece, before speakers and notes, both channels are read as 20 ms loudness frames. First the whole meeting: over the loud half of the frames where the system audio plays, the delay (−100…600 ms) at which microphone minus system audio (in dB) varies least. The speakers count as reaching the microphone only if that spread is at most 6 dB and at most half of what it is at a typical delay, and the microphone is above its noise floor (its quietest tenth, +10 dB) in at least half of those frames; with headphones no delay stands out and nothing is touched. Then each "Me" piece that an "Others" piece overlaps: a frame more than 6 dB above the echo the system audio predicts (the loudest system frame from 20 ms before to 80 ms after the delay, times the meeting's echo gain) is the user, the rest above the noise floor is echo. Under 0.5 s of echo: left alone. Under 0.5 s of the user: the whole piece is echo. Otherwise the words it shares, in order, with the overlapping "Others" text are cut (letters and digits only, width- and case-folded; runs of 4 CJK characters or 8 letters or more, with a misheard word between two runs cut too); under 3 s of the user with half its text shared, or nothing left, the whole piece is echo. A whole piece stays in `segments.json` with `"echo": true` and is left out of the transcript, the notes and Speaker Names; a trimmed one keeps what was transcribed in `textWithEcho`. The panel says "Removed N echoes: your microphone picked up the other side from the speakers." Older `segments.json` have neither field and read as no echo. | `MeetingEcho`, `MeetingRecorder.finishMeeting` |
| Remote speakers | After the echo step, only if "Others" spoke and the meeting is at least 15 s: `system.wav` is diarized once, offline, with FluidAudio's `OfflineDiarizerManager` (VBx clustering). Each "Others" piece takes the speaker it overlaps most, numbered by first appearance in the transcript: `Others 1`, `Others 2` (`segments.json` gets a `remote` field; a speaker who never wins a piece gets no number, so there is no gap). Speakers with under 3 s of speech are ignored; with fewer than two real speakers, or on any failure or timeout (60 s + half the meeting), the lines stay plain "Others" and the panel says why in one line (too short or only one other person, as information; timed out, speaker model download failed or failed, as a warning). Nothing is said when it worked or when nobody on the other side spoke. The models (Segmentation, FBank, Embedding, PldaRho) download on first use into `<Yap support folder>/SpeakerModels`, never FluidAudio's own Application Support folder; the panel shows "Downloading the speaker model… n%". Me is never diarized. | `MeetingDiarizer`, `SpeakerLabels`, `SpeakerSplitSkip` |
| Speakers after saving | Diarizing takes about 0.5–0.6 s per minute of audio once the models are cached (31–36 s for an hour, see below), so it's waited for 10 s at most. If it isn't done by then, the meeting is saved with plain "Others" and the entry marked pending (`meetingSpeakerStatus` = `pending`), the notes are written from that transcript, and the result is shown. The panel and the History row say "Telling speakers apart…" (the download progress too), and Speaker Names… waits. The same diarization keeps running; when it's done, `segments.json` is written whole (atomically) and then the entry's transcript and status are saved together; if that save fails, `segments.json` is put back, so the file and the entry never disagree. The notes aren't rewritten: the panel says the transcript has the speakers now and Regenerate Notes will use them. A failure is kept on the entry (the `SpeakerSplitSkip` raw value): the panel says why as above, the History row says "Speakers not told apart" with the reason as its tooltip. A meeting deleted meanwhile is left alone; if its folder is gone, it's marked failed. If Yap quits first, the next launch (after recovering interrupted meetings) diarizes the pending ones again, one at a time, in the background. A new meeting can start meanwhile; only the panel of that meeting is updated. Recovery waits for the speakers instead (it's in the background anyway). | `MeetingSpeakers.swift`, `MeetingRowTools`, `HistoryView` |
| Notes | After the last piece: the mode's AI provider with a built-in "Meeting Notes" prompt (summary, decisions, action items with owner and due date, open questions; notes in the meeting's main language, English terms kept). The timeout is 30 s plus 1 s per 100 characters, up to 300 s, instead of dictation's 7 s. Transcripts over 12,000 characters are summarized in parts, and the parts' notes are merged. Yap Refine or no configured provider: transcript only, with a hint; a failed request: transcript only, and the panel says "The notes couldn't be written:" and why. Nothing said (no transcript lines): no notes, and the panel says so instead of showing an empty transcript, without Copy or Regenerate Notes. The panel and History show the notes rendered (headings, lists, to-do boxes); copy and export give the Markdown. **Regenerate Notes** (panel, and the expanded History row) runs the same summarizer again with the current mode's provider and the speakers' names, from `segments.json` without the failed pieces. While it runs the row shows "Writing notes…"; if it can't (AI enhancement off, no provider, Yap Refine, the request failed) it says why and the old notes stay. On success `enhancedText`, the model name and the request are replaced. History's "re-enhance with a prompt" is hidden for meetings (player and quick history), since a dictation prompt would overwrite the notes. | `MeetingSummarizer`, `MeetingNotes`, `MeetingEdits`, `MeetingRowTools` |
| Saving | One `Transcription` with `kind = "meeting"`: `text` is the timestamped transcript (`[00:12] Me: …`), `enhancedText` the notes, `audioFileURL` the mix of both channels. The folder `Recordings/meetings/<id>/` also holds `mic.wav`, `system.wav` and `segments.json`. Deleting the entry, or audio retention, removes the whole folder. History can export a meeting as Markdown. **Speaker Names…** in the expanded History row names Me / Others / Others n (key `me`, `others`, `others-n`). The names are stored on the entry (`meetingSpeakerNamesJSON`; older entries have none), the transcript is rebuilt from `segments.json` with them (timestamps and pieces unchanged, `segments.json` untouched), and the export uses the new transcript. The notes aren't rewritten; the row offers Regenerate Notes, whose prompt then says who the user is and what the others are called. If saving the entry fails, the panel shows the error, where the audio is with Show in Finder, and no Open History; if writing the Markdown file fails, it shows that error. In History a meeting's row is tagged Meeting and its tabs are Transcript (plain text, one line per piece) and Notes (转写, 纪要), the words the panel uses; a meeting where nothing was said, or recovered with its audio only, has no meeting tools. History doesn't offer Retranscribe for a meeting (see the limits). | `MeetingRecorder.finishMeeting`, `Transcription.removeAudio` |
| Recovery | At launch, a folder in `Recordings/meetings/` that no History entry refers to and that isn't being recorded or finished right now (a crash or quit mid-meeting, or a failed save) is finished the same way as a meeting that was stopped: transcribed from `mic.wav` / `system.wav`, speakers, notes, one History entry dated when the meeting started. It runs in the background, one folder at a time, and never uses the panel, so a new meeting can start meanwhile. A notification says it started and how it ended. The originals are moved aside as `*.orig` while the folder is rewritten and deleted only after the entry is saved. With no transcription model in the mode, or when the previous launch's attempt was cut off (so a crash in recovery can't loop), the entry is saved with the audio only, status Failed, and the reason as its text. A saved folder is never recovered again. | `MeetingRecovery.swift` |
| Result panel | Above the notes, one line per thing to say, in the order of `MeetingStatusLine`: errors (red, ⚠: not saved, Markdown not written), then warnings (orange, ⚠: parts not transcribed, no notes and why, notes not regenerated, speakers not told apart after a failure, notes still saying "Others"), then information (secondary text, ⓘ or a spinner: telling speakers apart, writing notes, echo removed, too short or one other person). The rule is in `docs/DESIGN.md` › 会议的状态行; the History row's tools use the same lines. | `MeetingPanel.swift` |
| Consent | The first time, the panel explains what's recorded and that the user must tell everyone. "Copy Recording Notice" copies a two-language line for the meeting chat. While recording, the panel shows "Recording meeting" with a timer, and the menu bar shows ● and the time. Only ✓ in the panel ends a meeting; while recording, the menu item is "Show Meeting Panel". | `MeetingPanel.swift` |
| Call detection | Off unless turned on, or unless the user has recorded a meeting before (accepted the consent note): Settings › Meetings › **Remind Me to Record When a Call Starts** (next to the Record Meeting shortcut) (key `meetingCallDetection`; unset means "on if `meetingRecordingConsentShown`", so people who only dictate aren't bothered). Core Audio's process objects say which processes are running audio input: one listener on `kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList`, one on each process's `kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunningInput`, no polling; a one-shot timer runs only until the next 5 s / 10 s deadline. After sleep the listeners are set up again. Each process is recognized by its bundle ID or, through macOS's "responsible process" (`responsibility_get_pid_responsible_for_pid`, looked up at run time; it has no public header), the app it works for, so Chrome's helpers count as Chrome and WebKit's GPU process counts as Safari only when Safari owns it. Recognized: Zoom, Microsoft Teams (new and classic), Webex, Slack, Discord, FaceTime (its audio runs in `avconferenced`), Skype, Feishu, Lark, Tencent Meeting, VooV Meeting, DingTalk, and the browsers Chrome, Safari, Arc, Edge, Firefox and Brave (the table in `MeetingCallApp`). Yap's own process never counts. When one of them has used the microphone for 5 s and Yap isn't recording, finishing or asking for consent, isn't dictating and shows no other notification (the prompt then waits for it to go away), a notification says "Zoom is on a call. Record this meeting?" (a browser: "Google Chrome is using the microphone…", since it could be anything) with **Record Meeting**, the same as the shortcut (consent note first the first time); it stays 15 s. Once per call: the call is over, and the next one is asked about, after its app has left the microphone for 10 s. While recording, the call apps on the microphone when it started or later are watched; 10 s after the last of them left it, a notification says the call seems to have ended, with **Show Meeting Panel**. Nothing starts or stops by itself. | `MeetingCallDetector.swift` |

Permission: `NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription` ("System Audio Recording Only"). macOS asks the first time the tap is used. There's no API to query the answer: when it's denied, the tap still runs but delivers only zeros. If system audio is all zeros 8 s in, Yap says so, with a button to the Privacy settings. The message allows for the call simply not having started.

Agents (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex…) can read saved meetings through Yap's local, read-only MCP server, `Yap.app/Contents/Helpers/yap-mcp`, once Settings › Agent Access (MCP) › Let Agents Read Yap's Data is on (it's off by default): a list, search, and each meeting as the same Markdown as History's export (`MeetingMarkdown.swift`, compiled into both). See `docs/mcp.md`.

## Saving meetings to a folder

Select meetings in History (dictations may be selected too) and click **Save N Meetings…** in the selection bar; it counts the meetings only and isn't there when no meeting is selected. The sheet that opens (Save Meetings to Folder) says how many meetings, how many selected dictations are left out, that files are never replaced (a changed meeting gets a new file next to the old one), and that the files are separate from History (deleting a meeting or the retention period doesn't remove them; any app that can open the folder reads them; a syncing folder uploads them). **Choose Folder…** opens the folder picker; cancelling it writes nothing. Then the sheet lists what happened: new files, files already there with the same content, names taken by something else (left as they are, each with its file name and why), and failures (at most five names, then how many more), with the folder's path and Show in Finder. Code: `MeetingArchive.swift` (files), `MeetingArchiveSheet.swift` (button and sheet).

- **Content.** Each file is `MeetingNotes.markdown(for:)`, the bytes History's Export Markdown and yap-mcp's `get_meeting` give in the same language, locale and time zone: current names, notes, failed-piece lines. No front matter.
- **Names.** `2026-09-30 1405 meeting-<meeting id>-<first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the file>.md`: when the meeting started (not the day of the export) in the Mac's time zone, its id (the one `get_meeting` takes), and the version. Nothing from the transcript or speaker names goes into a name. Two meetings on one day get two names; saving the same meeting with the same bytes again adds nothing.
- **Versions, not sync.** Renaming speakers, regenerating the notes, or saving with another app language, locale or time zone changes the bytes (the heading, the date line), so the meeting gets a new file and the old one stays. The folder can hold several versions of one meeting; nothing marks the newest except the time the file was made.
- **Nothing is replaced or deleted.** For each name Yap first looks without following links: a regular file with the same bytes counts as already there; a file with other bytes (an edited copy), a symbolic link, a folder or anything else is left alone and reported. Otherwise it writes a hidden temporary file in that folder, `fsync`s it and renames it with `renameatx_np(RENAME_EXCL)`, which fails instead of replacing a file someone created meanwhile (then the name is compared like above). Where the volume has no exclusive rename, a hard link (`linkat`), which also fails on an existing name; never a plain rename. The temporary file is removed either way.
- **Folders.** The chosen folder is opened once; if it's gone by then, every meeting fails with "The folder isn't there anymore" and it isn't created again. A folder Yap can't write to fails each meeting, except copies already there with the same bytes. Nothing is rolled back: what was written stays when a later file fails, and the sheet says which. History and the meetings' audio are only read.

### Automatically, from Settings

Settings › Meetings › **Save Meetings to a Folder Automatically** is off until the user turns it on; the first time, it asks for a folder (cancelling leaves it off and changes nothing), and **Choose Folder…** next to the folder's path changes it later (cancelling changes nothing). While it's on, every time a meeting is saved in History its file is added to that folder, with the same content, names and no-replace rules as above (`MeetingAutoArchive`, writing through `MeetingArchive.export`).

- **Which saves.** All go through `MeetingEdits.save`: a meeting that ended (or was recovered at launch), its speakers saved later (`applySpeakers`, including a launch's `resumeSpeakers`), Speaker Names…, and Regenerate Notes once new notes were saved. A save that fails (and is rolled back) hands nothing on. Dictations never go there. Only once `context.save()` returned does `MeetingEdits.save` take the snapshot (`MeetingArchive.Entry`: id, start, Markdown bytes) on the main actor, synchronously, and queue it; `MeetingRecorder.save` posts `transcriptionCreated` only after that. So the order is save → snapshot → `transcriptionCreated`; the retention cleanup reacts to `transcriptionCompleted` (dictations) or sweeps at launch, and either way finds the snapshot already taken. Nothing depends on which observer runs first.
- **Pending speakers aren't waited for.** A long meeting saved with its speakers still being told apart gets its file right away; when the speakers are saved, the changed Markdown ("Others 1", "Others 2") is a new version next to it. That's usually two files for a long meeting. Waiting instead would lose the meeting whenever its entry is deleted before the speakers arrive (`applySpeakers` then finds no entry and does nothing). When diarization fails, or the recording folder is gone by then, only the speaker status changes, the Markdown doesn't, and the save finds the same file already there.
- **Not a backfill.** Turning it on writes nothing: meetings already in History are copied only with Save N Meetings… in History. An old meeting changed after it's on (new names, new notes) is a new save, so that version is written. A save missed while it was off, or still queued when Yap quit, isn't made up later; nothing scans History for them.
- **Queue and switching.** Files are written one at a time on a serial queue, never on the main actor. Turning it off or choosing another folder first stops everything queued for the old destination (a generation counter checked before each file starts), then waits for the one file being written right now, and only then shows the switch off or the new folder: nothing is written for the old destination after that. Turning it off keeps the folder for next time.
- **What Settings shows.** Below the switch: the folder, that only meetings saved from now on are written and a meeting with pending speakers is saved twice, that a syncing folder uploads the files, then the last result of this session: waiting (with how many), saved (the file name), already there, a name taken by something else (edited copy, link…), or the error (folder gone, not allowed to write…), with Choose Folder… to pick another. No notification per meeting and no retries: the next save of a meeting tries again. A folder that's gone isn't created again, and nothing falls back to another folder. History and the audio are never changed by a failed write.
- **This Mac only.** The switch and the folder's path are two UserDefaults keys (`meetingAutoArchiveEnabled`, `meetingAutoArchiveFolder`) that no settings backup (Export Settings), config.json or Yap Cloud sync carries, and importing settings doesn't set them, so someone else's settings can't turn it on or point it elsewhere. Yap isn't sandboxed, so the path is kept as is, without a security-scoped bookmark.

## Verified here

`make meeting-files-check MODEL=<ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin> [NOTES=1]` runs the whole pipeline without any capture permission. It builds a one-minute test meeting from `setup/asr/clips`: the Tingting clips are "Me", the Reed clips "Others", taking turns. It launches the mock identity with `--meeting-files mic.wav system.wav` and prints the result. Results on 2026-09-26 (M4 Pro):

- 41 s per channel, done 7.7 s after "stop", with local Whisper Large v3 Turbo (Quantized) and deepseek-v4.1-flash notes.
- Transcript lines interleave correctly: `[00:00] Me`, `[00:08] Others`, `[00:21] Others`, `[00:28] Me`.
- The notes have headings 摘要 / 决定 / 待办 / 未决问题, English terms are kept, and an owner and due date come out where they were said (Sara, 周四).
- Without `NOTES=1` the mode has AI enhancement off: the transcript is saved with the hint, and no model (local Ollama included) is started.
- Written to disk: `mic.wav mix.wav segments.json system.wav`.

Failures and recovery, 2026-09-30 (same script, which now always runs these): the first piece is made to fail (`--meeting-fail-pieces 1`, DEBUG only; a real failure can't be produced on demand) and the entry's save too (`--meeting-fail-save`). The transcript keeps exactly one marked line and the result counts 1. Then a folder cut off by a crash (both WAV headers with size 0, a leftover `pieces/`) is put next to the unsaved one, and the app is launched twice with `--meeting-recovery-check`: the first launch recovers both as transcribed meetings (`mix.wav`, `segments.json`, no `*.orig` or `pieces/` left), the second recovers nothing. A third folder, marked as a recovery that was cut off last time (in the attempted list, `mic.wav.orig` still aside), is saved with its audio only and the reason.

Speaker labels, 2026-09-29, same script with a second remote voice (Shelley says two lines between Reed's, made with `say`, 50 s per channel, Whisper base-q5_1 for speed): the two voices get `Others 1` and `Others 2` and the check (`labels: OK`, read from `segments.json`) requires one label per voice. Diarizing took 14.5 s and 32 s in two cold runs that included downloading the models. Nothing was written to `~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio`. Since 2026-09-30 this run waits for the speakers (`--meeting-speaker-wait 600`, because its save is made to fail and a meeting saved without them would never get them) and requires `echo-removed 0`: its microphone is silent while the others speak, so nothing may be taken out (`echo coupling none: spread 3.3 dB, typical 3.3, heard 0%`).

Echo, 2026-09-30, `make meeting-echo-check MODEL=<ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin>` (M4 Pro): a 93 s meeting from the clips and `say` (Tingting is "Me"; Reed, Shelley and Eddy are "Others"; Tingting talks over Reed once, for 8 s), with room noise at −60 dBFS in the microphone, run three times: without echo; with the system audio 30 ms later and 12 dB quieter in the microphone; and 80 ms later, 20 dB quieter, plus a second reflection 35 ms after that, 9 dB below it. The check compares, per line, how much of what was said is in the kept "Me" text (letters in order, in runs of 3 or more).

- Without echo nothing is taken out: no delay stands out (spread 3.6 dB against 3.7 typical, microphone heard in 14 % of the loud frames).
- With echo the delay is found (40 ms: spread 2.3 dB against 21.6; 80 ms: 1.1 against 12.5) and 3 of the 4 "Me" pieces are trimmed. None is all echo: each 20–28 s piece also holds some of the user.
- What the others said while the user was quiet had been transcribed under "Me" (up to 95 % of a line) and is at 0–16 % after. What's left is echo Whisper heard differently from the system channel (for Shelley's line: "Huberless那边的Doll PTA才7 自断" instead of "Kubernetes 那边的 rollout…").
- Every line the user said keeps what was transcribed of it; the one said over Reed keeps 65 % (near) and 81 % (far), against 85 % without echo. The drop comes from transcribing with the echo underneath, not from the trimming.
- The check needs Large v3 Turbo. With Whisper base-q5_1 (2026-09-30) it fails: base transcribes little of the echo under "Me" in the first place ("the echo was never transcribed under Me, so this run checks nothing") and drops some of the user's own lines, so there is nothing to compare.

Long meetings, 2026-09-30, `make meeting-long-check MODEL=<ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin>` on an Apple M4 Pro (Mac16,7), Debug build: an 11-minute meeting, "Me" and three remote voices, runs through the whole pipeline, twice in full.

- Telling speakers apart took 16.3 s and 16.8 s cold (downloading the models first) and 5.6 s and 6.6 s warm. Warm that is 0.51–0.60 s per minute of audio, 31–36 s for an hour; cold about 1.5 s per minute. An hour's meeting would keep the result waiting well past 10 s, hence speakers after saving (above).
- Transcribing with Large v3 Turbo (Quantized) took 2.4–3.0 s per piece in three runs (63 pieces of 20–28 s, both channels, one at a time: 153.9 s, 164.1 s and 191.0 s), about 0.12 × real time. One cold run took 737 s (11.7 s per piece), unexplained; the cold run before it took 164 s. During a meeting the pieces are transcribed as they come, so at "stop" only the last piece of each channel should be left: by the per-piece rate, about 5–6 s before the speakers and the notes (an estimate; stopping a live meeting wasn't timed here).
- Saved before the speakers were ready (`--meeting-speaker-wait 0`): the entry had plain "Others" and `pending`; 4.8 s after saving the same entry had `Others 1` and `Others 2`, no status, and the panel dropped "Telling speakers apart…" for the Regenerate Notes hint.
- Quit before the speakers were ready (the first 3 minutes, `--meeting-exit-before-speakers`): the entry stayed `pending`; the next launch (`--meeting-speakers-resume-check`) resumed that one meeting and it got `Others 1` and `Others 2`.

Self-checks at launch cover:
- call detection (`MeetingCallPolicy`, a pure function of timed microphone snapshots and Yap's state): the 5 s debounce, once per call and again after 10 s off, not while recording, in the consent note, finishing or dictating, a notification on screen defers the prompt without dropping it, Yap's own process and unknown bundle IDs never count, helpers and WebKit's GPU process by their responsible app, whole bundle IDs only, the browser's wording, the meeting app named before the browser, the 10 s end debounce, once per end, apps that join during the recording, nothing after the meeting ended;
- cutting and dropping pieces, the timeline, and the WAV writer;
- the transcript and Markdown format (also without the transcript, as yap-mcp's `include_transcript: false` gives it), the speakers read back from a saved transcript (yap-mcp's `list_meetings`; timestamps `[m:ss]`, `[mm:ss]` and `[h:mm:ss]` only, a name up to the first ": "), splitting long transcripts, the timeout;
- a notes request that timed out says "The request timed out. Check your connection and try again." (`MeetingSummarizer.failureDescription`), not dictation cleanup's "Enhancement request timed out… increase the timeout duration" (润色请求超时), whose setting notes don't use; other failures keep the shared wording;
- mapping diarizer turns to `Others n` (order, noise speakers, one speaker, no overlap, no number for a speaker who wins no piece, old `segments.json`);
- echo (`MeetingEcho`): the user's frames only 6 dB above the predicted echo and above the noise floor; the noise floor; shared text in Chinese and English (width, case and punctuation folded; 4 CJK characters or 8 letters; a misheard word between two runs), cutting it without dangling punctuation; each decision boundary (0.5 s of echo, 0.5 s and 3 s of the user, half the text, nothing left); the delay and gain found in a synthetic echo and none with headphones; the transcript without echo pieces; `echo` / `textWithEcho` read back and an old `segments.json` without them;
- speakers after saving: turns to labels or the reason, which reasons are kept on the entry as failures, the stored status read back;
- the right-⌘ rule;
- the result panel's status lines: errors, then warnings, then information, whatever happens together; which speaker reasons are warnings; regenerating hides the last failure; nothing said can't be regenerated; a saved transcript told from the "nothing was said" text and a recovery reason in any language (`MeetingNotes.hasLines`);
- Settings search: the meeting settings (the shortcut, the call reminder, saving to a folder automatically) are found in Meetings, not Additional Shortcuts;
- deleting a meeting's folder;
- the failed-piece line, why speakers weren't told apart, and which folders count as interrupted (not one with an entry, not the meeting being recorded or finished);

## Checks that need real permissions (to do by hand)

None of these can be run here without UI automation or permission prompts on the real desktop. Each can be checked afterwards from the files in the meeting's folder (`~/Library/Application Support/me.sma1lboy.yap/Recordings/meetings/<id>/`) or from History.

1. **The tap records.** Start a meeting recording (right ⌘ + Space) while a video plays in Safari and a call runs in Chrome. The first time, macOS asks for System Audio Recording: allow it. Talk a bit, stop after a minute.
   - Expected: `system.wav` has the video or call and none of Yap's own sounds; `mic.wav` has your voice; History shows "Others" lines.
2. **Permission denied.** Reset with `tccutil reset AudioCapture <bundle id>`, start again and deny.
   - Expected: recording continues, and within about 8 s a notice says there's no sound from other apps, with Open Settings; `system.wav` is silent.
   - Also check which Settings pane the button opens (it uses the Screen & System Audio Recording URL).
3. **Headphones switch.** During a recording, connect or disconnect AirPods (and switch the output in Control Center).
   - Expected: the recording continues. `system.wav` keeps recording after the switch, with a silent gap of about the switch time, and both files stay about the same length.
   - The microphone is not switched: if the input device disappears (AirPods were also the mic), "Me" goes silent for the rest of the meeting. See the limits below.
4. **Dictation during a meeting.** While recording, dictate once.
   - Expected: the call stays audible (no output mute) and isn't paused, and the dictation pastes as usual.
5. **Shortcut vs Spotlight.**
   - Left ⌘ + Space still opens Spotlight.
   - Right ⌘ + Space toggles meeting recording and does not open Spotlight.
   - After recording a different shortcut in Settings › Meetings › Record Meeting, only that one works.
6. **The panel doesn't take focus.** With a call or document focused, start and stop a recording.
   - Expected: typing keeps going to the other app, and the buttons in the panel still work.
7. **A long meeting.** One real meeting of 30–60 minutes.
   - Watch that transcription keeps up (pieces are 20–28 s).
   - Watch how long the notes take: a transcript over 12,000 characters is summarized in parts.
   - Disk use is about 115 MB per channel per hour, plus the mix.
   - After ✓: the result is up within a few seconds after the notes, with "Telling speakers apart…"; within about a minute the transcript gets Others 1, Others 2 (panel and History) and the panel suggests Regenerate Notes. Quit Yap while it still says "Telling speakers apart…" and start it again: the same History entry gets the speakers.
8. **Call detection.** Turn on Settings › Meetings › Remind Me to Record When a Call Starts.
   - Start a FaceTime call (or join a Zoom meeting, or a Google Meet in Chrome) and run `make meeting-call-check` while it runs: the call's process is listed as a call app (FaceTime shows as `com.apple.avconferenced`; Chrome as its helper, for pid … `com.google.Chrome`). Log lines under the `MeetingCallDetector` category say when a prompt was shown.
   - About 5 s into the call, the notification "FaceTime is on a call. Record this meeting?" appears (for Chrome: "Google Chrome is using the microphone. Record this meeting?"). Record Meeting starts the recording (the consent note first, the first time). Ignoring it: no second prompt during the same call.
   - Hang up while recording: about 10 s later, "The call seems to have ended. Click ✓ to end the meeting and save it." appears, and the recording keeps running until ✓.
   - Dictate while a call starts: no prompt while dictating; it comes after the dictation. With the switch off, nothing appears.
   - Put the Mac to sleep during a call and wake it: a new call still prompts.
9. **Without headphones.** A call on the Mac's own speakers and microphone, both sides talking, at least once at the same time.
   - Expected: the panel says "Removed N echoes…"; the "Me" lines have what you said and not the other side's sentences (some misheard fragments can stay); what you said while they talked is still there.
   - `segments.json` has `"echo": true` on whole echo pieces and `textWithEcho` on trimmed ones; compare them with the "Others" lines at the same time. The same call with headphones: nothing removed.

## Known limits

- **Timestamps are per piece** (20–28 s), not per sentence. One piece can hold two turns of the same speaker.
- **A piece with two remote speakers gets one label**, the one who spoke longer in it: pieces are cut at pauses, not at speaker changes, and the text can't be split without word timestamps. The diarizer only numbers them, and the numbers mean nothing in the next meeting; names are given by hand in History (see below).
- **Echo is taken out afterwards, as far as the two channels and the text show it.** Without headphones, "Me" pieces that are the other side coming out of the speakers are left out, and the other side's words are cut from pieces where the user spoke too. Without word timestamps a trimmed piece is cut by its text: words Whisper heard differently in the echo than on the system channel stay under "Me" (in the check, garbled bits of the echo inside pieces where the user also spoke), and if the user repeats what the other side says at the same moment, that's cut too. A room so loud or reverberant, or speakers so distorted, that the microphone doesn't follow the system audio at one delay gets nothing removed. macOS's echo cancellation while recording (voice processing, `setVoiceProcessingEnabled`) isn't used: it changes how the microphone sounds, turns other apps' audio down during the meeting, and can't be checked from files.
- **Speakers of a long meeting arrive after its notes.** Past 10 s the meeting is saved with plain "Others" and the notes say "Others"; Regenerate Notes uses the speakers once they're in. Echo and speakers aren't redone by Regenerate Notes or Speaker Names.
- **A meeting and dictation on different local Whisper models take turns, one model in memory.** Up to 1.13.0 they freed each other's model and pieces failed. Now nothing fails, but every switch between the two is a model load: a dictation during the meeting waits for the piece being decoded plus its model's load (1.6 s instead of 0.5–0.6 s in `make isolation-check` with Large v3 Turbo and Base), and pieces queue meanwhile. Using the same model for both avoids it. A dictation that waits more than a second says so in the recorder ("Waiting for a meeting piece", the step and its time; see [what a wait shows](local-models.md#what-a-wait-shows)). FluidAudio models take the same kind of turns (one transcription or release at a time per service); transcribe.cpp runs one native chunk at a time per model. Meetings on those two weren't run.
- **Pieces are transcribed in the background without App Nap.** While a local model works (a piece, a dictation, an import), Yap holds a user-initiated activity so macOS doesn't throttle it with no Yap window in front; without it, speech detection slowed 6–8 times partway through a background run of `make lifecycle-check` (details in [local-models.md](local-models.md#why-one-run-was-20-times-slower)). That run had no meeting in it.
- **A meeting counts as work for "Keep model loaded".** While recording or finishing, neither the idle timer nor a memory-pressure warning frees the local model between two pieces (it used to, and the next piece loaded it again); the release happens after the meeting ends. In the final `make lifecycle-check` Debug residency run, the model was released about 1.72 s after the meeting ended, with 0 failed pieces. The earlier 15 s figure was an observation limit reached without a release, not a successful release time; see [the run details](local-models.md#keep-model-loaded-against-real-work).
- **End Meeting and Quit waits for the remaining pieces and the notes.** In `make quit-check` (`meeting` case, a 54 s meeting fed from files, Large v3 Turbo, no notes) it saved the meeting with 0 failed pieces and exited 5.5–15 s after Quit in three runs. Capture itself (microphone, system audio permission) isn't exercised by that case. With the menu bar badge as it was in 1.13.0 the same case never quit within a minute (twice): the elapsed time was a `TimelineView` that redrew the status item without pause for the whole meeting (a sample of the main thread: 99% in `MenuBarExtraController.updateButton`), and pieces and the Quit waited behind it; it now ticks from a one-second timer.
- **Microphone device changes aren't followed.** Only the system audio side is rebuilt; the timeline stays aligned, but "Me" is lost after the input disappears.
- **Recovery is from the files only.** A meeting cut off by a crash is transcribed again from the start at the next launch (the pieces already transcribed weren't kept), so it takes about as long as finishing it would have. Its time is the folder's creation time. A folder with no audio at all (a crash in the first second) is left alone.
- **No "Retranscribe" for meetings.** Rerunning a meeting as one dictation would drop the two channels, the speakers and the notes, and rerunning the meeting steps would have to rewrite the notes too; History hides the action for meetings (row menu, player, quick history) and the service refuses a meeting's audio.
- **Names are per meeting.** Nothing carries a name to the next meeting, and renaming needs the meeting's folder (it's gone after audio retention). Names aren't asked for in the panel; they're set in History.
- **Notes need the mode's AI enhancement switch on.** With it off (a fresh install's default), the transcript is saved with a hint and no model, local ones included, is started.
- **Storage and cost:** WAV only, no m4a. A cloud transcription model is called once per piece, which for an hour-long meeting means 150–250 billed calls (Yap Cloud included).
- **Saved folders hold versions, not one file per meeting.** Each change of names, notes, language, locale or time zone adds a file the next time the meeting is saved there; old versions and edited copies are never cleaned up by Yap. The names are long (start time, the full meeting id, 12 hex digits) so that they can't collide and agents can match them to `get_meeting`; in a notes app they sort by date but aren't titles. Saving automatically makes this more visible: a long meeting whose speakers arrive after it was saved gets two files (without and with "Others 1/2"), and every rename or regenerate adds one.
- **Saving automatically is best effort, not a backup.** It writes what's saved while it's on, and only then: saves still queued when Yap quits, made while it was off, or failed (folder gone, no permission, a name taken) are not tried again until that meeting is saved again; Save N Meetings… in History writes them. The last result is shown in Settings for this session only. The folder picker, Finder and VoiceOver on the new Settings rows weren't operated here (no desktop automation); the rows were checked in `make ui-snapshots` and the behavior in `make meeting-archive-check`.
- **Saving to a volume without exclusive rename or hard links** (some network shares, FAT/exFAT drives) fails each file with the system's error rather than risk replacing one. Not tried here on such a volume.
- **Call detection sees the microphone, not the call.** A browser, Slack or Discord using the microphone for anything (a voice message, a dictation site, a huddle) looks like a call, which is why a browser's prompt only says it's using the microphone. An app that releases the microphone when muted makes a long mute look like the end of the call (the reminder never stops anything). FaceTime is recognized by the `avconferenced` daemon, which also carries iPhone calls relayed to the Mac and other audio calls. None of the apps was seen in a real call here: the bundle IDs of Chrome, Safari, Arc, Firefox, Discord, Lark and FaceTime (and their helpers') were read from the apps installed on this Mac; the others come from their Homebrew casks (Webex, Tencent Meeting, VooV Meeting, DingTalk, Feishu) or published lists (Zoom, Teams, Slack, Skype, Edge, Brave, `avconferenced` for FaceTime calls).
